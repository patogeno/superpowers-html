Brainstorming shows you the experience first: what users can do, the screens as a clickable prototype, and how the solution flows. On Claude Code it is all one artifact you can review from your PC or phone. Technical design stays one tap away, folded.

1. What it's for

Why. Claude now gets the technical design right often enough that you rarely review it. What you do review is the experience: the features users get, the screens, and the flow of the app. Today's spec leads with architecture and data-flow diagrams and keeps mockups as local files you can only open at your desk.

Goals

  • Every design review starts with what users can do, what they see, and how they move through it.
  • You can review from anywhere, PC or phone, by clicking through a prototype, commenting on it, or writing a general note in chat.
  • Technical design is still there, complete, for when you want it, but it's optional and folded by default.
  • High-level solution flows (the "half-technical" view) are shown by default, so you can see intent and shape the flows.
  • Take upstream Superpowers v7.0.2, including its rebuilt brainstorming, without losing any of the above.

Non-goals: changing how plans are executed, or keeping the server-based visual companion running.

It fails if: you have to open the technical section to understand what gets built, a review on your phone breaks or scrolls sideways, or feedback you left as a comment gets missed.

2. What you can do

  • See ideas as they come upWhenever something is easier to see than read, Claude publishes it as an artifact mid-conversation and gives you the link. It doesn't ask first. See it
  • Read the spec experience-firstPurpose, features, screens, flows, approaches, in that order. See it
  • Click through the prototypeA second page in the same artifact, styled like the product, with working navigation. Every screen in the spec opens the prototype on that screen. See it
  • Open the technical design when you want itBlock diagrams, data model, and the technical calls Claude made, folded at the bottom of the spec. See it
  • Comment on what you looked atLeave comments on either page from the claude.ai viewer, PC or phone. See it
  • Or just write a note in chatRushing? Tell Claude your general preferences. Claude reads the comments and your note together, answers each comment, revises, and republishes to the same link. Anything you didn't touch counts as approved. See it
  • Approve onceApproving the spec and prototype is the last checkpoint. The plan is written and executed without another review unless planning finds a design problem.

Outside Claude Code there's no artifact viewer, so the same two pages are saved in the topic folder and you open them locally. Comments aren't available there; feedback goes in chat.

4. How it flows

The review loop. It runs as many times as you need; each pass republishes to the same link.

Anything you didn't comment on or mention counts as approved when you say go.

ApproachTrades off

Calls Claude made

  • Claude's call The spec template becomes a page fragment. The artifact viewer wraps the main page in its own document shell, and browsers open a fragment locally too. The prototype stays a full document, because supporting pages aren't wrapped.
  • Claude's call A topic folder's main page is index.html (was design.html), with prototype.html beside it. The artifact serves its main page as index.html, so the prototype's "Back to spec" link works both in the artifact and from local files. A spec with no prototype stays a single …-design.html file.
  • Claude's call Visuals mid-conversation are published without asking, replacing upstream's "offer the companion" message: a link is cheap to ignore, and upstream already treats "how to show something" as Claude's call.
  • Claude's call Comments are read when you reply (and from watch notices if the session gets them), not polled. Each comment gets an answer in its own thread.
  • Claude's call Untouched parts count as approved only when you give a go-ahead. A batch of comments alone is feedback, not approval.
  • Claude's call Small changes keep upstream's short in-chat design; they get a prototype artifact only when they change something visible.
  • Claude's call Prototype screen ids avoid the names claude.ai reserves (settings, sidebar, comment-*, …).
  • Claude's call The fork's version becomes 7.1.0 (upstream 7.0.2 plus this feature).
  • Claude's call The architecture and data-flow diagrams are still required for a project, but inside the technical section; the default view requires the features, screens and flow diagram instead.
